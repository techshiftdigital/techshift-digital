What is the Roman Numeral Converter?

Convert whole numbers to Roman numerals or Roman numerals to decimal values. Use it for a quick, transparent result, then review the method and boundaries below before relying on the answer.

Formula

Roman numerals use additive and subtractive symbols: I=1, V=5, X=10, L=50, C=100, D=500 and M=1000.

The calculator keeps full precision during the calculation and rounds the displayed result for readability.

Practical example

2026 converts to MMXXVI; XLIV converts to 44.

How to use this in Excel

Place the inputs in the cells referenced below, enter the formula, and apply the appropriate number format.

=ROMAN(A2) converts a number to Roman numerals; =ARABIC(A2) converts back in supported Excel versions

Review units and cell references before copying the formula down a worksheet.

Common mistakes and limitations

  • Writing IIII instead of IV in standard notation.
  • Entering zero or negative values.
  • Assuming every historical source follows identical rules.

Important limitation: The conventional range is 1–3999. Historical notation varies, and zero has no standard Roman numeral.
