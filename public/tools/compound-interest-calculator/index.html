What is the Compound Interest Calculator?

Project how an initial balance grows with compound interest and regular contributions. Use it for a quick, transparent result, then review the method and boundaries below before relying on the answer.

Formula

Future value = P(1+r/m)^(mt), plus the future value of regular contributions when supplied.

The calculator keeps full precision during the calculation and rounds the displayed result for readability.

Practical example

$10,000 invested at 7% compounded monthly for 10 years grows to about $20,097 without additional contributions.

How to use this in Excel

Place the inputs in the cells referenced below, enter the formula, and apply the appropriate number format.

=FV(B2/12,C2*12,-D2,-A2)

Review units and cell references before copying the formula down a worksheet.

Common mistakes and limitations

  • Confusing nominal and effective annual rates.
  • Entering monthly contributions as annual amounts.
  • Treating projections as guaranteed returns.

Important limitation: Returns are assumed constant, which is unrealistic for most investments. Taxes, fees and inflation are excluded.
